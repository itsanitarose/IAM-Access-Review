## 2. How I Rated the Risks

I considered how likely the access was to be misused and how much harm that could cause, including the permissions involved and the sensitivity of patient data. My approach was informed by the [OWASP Risk Rating Methodology](https://community.owasp.org/OWASP_Risk_Rating_Methodology).

## 3. What Needs Attention First

### First Priority: `treyes`

The recorded contract end date was 31 July 2026, but an Admin login occurred on 28 September. I put this account first because it had Admin access, so misuse could cause more damage.

**Potential governance gap:** The records provided did not confirm whether the contract had been extended or whether continued Admin access was approved. This did not prove the account was compromised, but it left the reason for keeping that access unclear.

Recommended next steps:

- Confirm whether the contract was extended.
- Check whether Admin access is still authorised.
- If no valid authorisation exists, disable access and investigate the activity.

This approach follows [NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/53/r5/upd1/final), AC-2 Account Management, which calls for access to be based on valid authorisation and for accounts to be reviewed. AC-2(3), Disable Accounts, also supports disabling accounts when they have expired or are no longer associated with a user.

### What the Records Don’t Tell Us

These logins need investigating, but they do not prove who used the accounts, whether patient information was accessed, or whether a breach occurred.
